Dimensionality Reduction 09-UMAP Lesson 9.4 Advanced 15 mins

Fuzzy Cross-Entropy Loss

Lesson Objectives

What you will learn

Understand UMAP's low-dimensional Student-t similarity ($q_{ij}$) and the fuzzy cross-entropy loss function that drives layout optimization.

Prerequisites

Lessons 9.2-9.3 on asymmetric similarity and symmetrization; basic understanding of KL divergence.

Expected Outcome

Explain why UMAP's cross-entropy loss preserves global structure while t-SNE's KL divergence does not.

Equation 3: Low-Dimensional Similarity (Student-t Approximation)

In the 2D projected space, UMAP defines the similarity between the coordinates $y_i$ and $y_j$ using a heavy-tailed curve:

q_{ij} = \left( 1 + a \cdot \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^{2b} \right)^{-1}

Symbols Legend

SymbolMathematical MeaningConceptual Role
$q_{ij}$Low-dimensional similarityThe similarity of points $i$ and $j$ on the 2D map.
$y_i, y_j$2D/3D coordinate vectorsThe target coordinates optimized during layout construction.
$a, b$Fitted hyper-parametersCurve-fitting constants adjusted to approximate a step function based on min_dist. For default min_dist=0.1, $a \approx 1.93$ and $b \approx 0.79$.
💡
The Student-t distribution has heavier tails than a Gaussian, meaning points far apart in 2D still retain a small non-zero similarity, which helps gradient flow during optimization.

Equation 4: Fuzzy Cross-Entropy Loss

UMAP optimizes the positions of the low-dimensional coordinates by minimizing the cross-entropy between the high-dimensional similarity $p_{ij}$ and the low-dimensional similarity $q_{ij}$:

\mathcal{L}_{CE} = \sum_{i \neq j} \left[ p_{ij} \log\left(\frac{p_{ij}}{q_{ij}}\right) + (1 - p_{ij}) \log\left(\frac{1 - p_{ij}}{1 - q_{ij}}\right) \right]

Why Cross-Entropy Beats KL Divergence

  • The Left Term (Attraction Force): active when $p_{ij} \approx 1$. If two points are neighbors in high-D, this term pulls their 2D coordinates close together.
  • The Right Term (Repulsion Force): active when $p_{ij} \approx 0$. If two points are not neighbors, this term pushes their 2D coordinates apart.
  • Contrast with t-SNE: t-SNE uses KL Divergence, which lacks the second term. Consequently, t-SNE focuses entirely on attraction, allowing clusters to drift anywhere on the map. UMAP's repulsion term preserves the global layout.
💡
The repulsion term is what gives UMAP its global structure preservation. Without it, UMAP would behave like t-SNE â€” local neighborhoods intact but global positioning meaningless.
