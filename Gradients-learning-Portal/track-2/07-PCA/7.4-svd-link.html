Lesson 7.4: The SVD and PCA Connection

While PCA is traditionally taught via eigendecomposition of the covariance matrix, most practical implementations â€” including Scikit-learn â€” use Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) under the hood. Understanding this connection deepens your grasp of both algorithms.

From Eigendecomposition to SVD

Recall that PCA finds eigenvectors of the covariance matrix:

\Sigma = \frac{1}{n-1} \tilde{X}^T \tilde{X}

Instead of forming $\Sigma$ explicitly (a $d \times d$ matrix), SVD works directly on the centered data matrix $\tilde{X}$ (size $n \times d$). The SVD of $\tilde{X}$ is:

\tilde{X} = U S V^T

Where:

  • $U$ is an $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix of left singular vectors
  • $S$ is an $n \times d$ diagonal matrix of singular values $s_i$
  • $V$ is a $d \times d$ orthogonal matrix of right singular vectors

The Key Connection

The right singular vectors $V$ are exactly the eigenvectors of the covariance matrix, and the singular values $s_i$ relate directly to the eigenvalues:

\tilde{X}^T \tilde{X} = V S^T U^T U S V^T = V S^T S V^T
\Sigma = \frac{1}{n-1} V \Lambda V^T \quad \text{where} \quad \lambda_i = \frac{s_i^2}{n-1}

In short: the eigenvectors of $X^T X$ are the right singular vectors of $X$, and the eigenvalues are the squared singular values divided by $n-1$.

Why SVD is Preferred

  • Numerical Stability: Forming the covariance matrix $X^T X$ squares the condition number, losing precision. SVD avoids this by operating directly on $X$.
  • Computational Efficiency: For tall-skinny matrices ($n \gg d$), SVD on $X$ is faster than eigendecomposition of $X^T X$.
  • No Covariance Matrix Needed: SVD can handle sparse and large datasets where $X^T X$ would be dense and memory-intensive.
  • Consistency: SVD is well-defined even when the covariance matrix is singular (rank-deficient), gracefully handling zero eigenvalues.

Putting It Together

To compute PCA projections via SVD:

  • Center the data: $\tilde{X} = X - \mu$
  • Compute SVD: $\tilde{X} = U S V^T$
  • Principal components: columns of $V$ (sorted by descending singular values)
  • Projected data: $Z = \tilde{X} V_k$ (keeping top $k$ columns of $V$)
