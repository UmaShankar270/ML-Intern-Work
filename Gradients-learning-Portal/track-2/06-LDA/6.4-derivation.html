Dimensionality Reduction 06-LDA Lesson 6.4 Advanced 15 mins

Mathematical Derivation of LDA

Lesson Objectives

What you will learn

Derive the LDA optimization objective, understand the determinant ratio, and learn the projection equation and eigenproblem.

Prerequisites

Completion of Lesson 6.3 (Scatter Matrices) â€” familiar with \( S_W \) and \( S_B \).

Expected Outcome

Solve the generalized eigenvalue problem \( S_W^{-1} S_B \) and project data into lower-dimensional space using the projection matrix.

Optimisation Objective

\[ \max_{W} \frac{\det(W^T S_B W)}{\det(W^T S_W W)} \]

  • \( \det \) means determinant â€” a single number that summarises the "volume" of a matrix. Think of it as a score for how spread out the data is in all directions at once.
  • \( W \) is a matrix whose columns are the projection directions (new axes) we are solving for.
  • The numerator \( \det(W^T S_B W) \) scores between-class separation after projection.
  • The denominator \( \det(W^T S_W W) \) scores within-class spread after projection.
  • We want the numerator large (classes far apart) and the denominator small (each class tight).
  • Practical significance: This is LDA's compass. Every direction \( W \) is scored by how much it separates classes divided by how much it scatters them. The top-\( k \) directions with the highest scores become our new axes.

Projection Equation

\[ Y = X W \]

  • \( X \): original data matrix (shape \( N \times d \)).
  • \( W \): projection matrix from LDA (shape \( d \times k \)).
  • \( Y \): reduced data (shape \( N \times k \)).
  • Practical significance: Multiply your data by the LDA directions and you get a lower-dimensional representation that is optimised for class separation.

The Generalized Eigenproblem

Maximizing the Fisher criterion leads to solving the generalized eigenvalue problem:

\[ S_W^{-1} S_B w = \lambda w \]
  • The eigenvectors \( w \) are the optimal projection directions (LDA components).
  • The eigenvalues \( \lambda \) represent the amount of class separation captured by each direction.
  • Sort eigenvectors by descending eigenvalues â€” the top-\( k \) directions form the columns of the projection matrix \( W \).
  • You obtain at most \( C - 1 \) non-zero eigenvalues because \( S_B \) has rank at most \( C - 1 \).
  • Practical significance: LDA reduces to a linear algebra problem â€” no iterative optimization or gradient descent is needed. The answer comes in closed form.
