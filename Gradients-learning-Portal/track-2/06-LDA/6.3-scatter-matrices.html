Scatter Matrices (Within and Between Class)

Lesson Objectives

What you will learn

Construct Within-Class (\( S_W \)) and Between-Class (\( S_B \)) scatter matrices and understand how they quantify class structure for LDA.

Prerequisites

Completion of Lesson 6.2 (Fisher's Criterion) â€” comfortable with class means and global means.

Expected Outcome

Manually compute scatter matrices and explain their practical significance in the LDA optimization objective.

Within-Class Scatter Matrix \( S_W \)

\[ S_W = \sum_{i=1}^{C} \sum_{j=1}^{N_i} (x_j^{(i)} - \mu_i)(x_j^{(i)} - \mu_i)^T \]

Measure how far each dog is from the average dog, do the same for cats and birds, and sum everything up.

  • \( (x_j^{(i)} - \mu_i) \): deviation of a single sample from its own class centre.
  • The product \( (x - \mu)(x - \mu)^T \) is a covariance-like matrix that captures spread in all directions.
  • Practical significance: \( S_W \) quantifies the "messiness" inside classes. LDA wants this to be small â€” tight clusters.

Between-Class Scatter Matrix \( S_B \)

\[ S_B = \sum_{i=1}^{C} N_i (\mu_i - \mu)(\mu_i - \mu)^T \]

Measure how far each class centre is from the global centre, weighted by class size.

  • \( (\mu_i - \mu) \): deviation of a class centre from the global centre.
  • Multiplied by \( N_i \) so larger classes have more influence.
  • Practical significance: \( S_B \) quantifies how far apart the groups are. LDA wants this to be large â€” well-separated classes.

Optimisation Objective

Together, \( S_W \) and \( S_B \) form LDA's objective function:

\[ \max_{W} \frac{\det(W^T S_B W)}{\det(W^T S_W W)} \]
  • The numerator uses \( S_B \) to score between-class separation after projection.
  • The denominator uses \( S_W \) to score within-class spread after projection.
  • LDA searches for projection directions \( W \) that make this ratio as large as possible â€” keeping classes tight (small \( S_W \)) and far apart (large \( S_B \)).
