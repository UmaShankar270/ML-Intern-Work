Expected Outcome

Identify imbalanced classification scenarios and select Stratified K-Fold to ensure representative minority-class evaluation.

2. Stratified K-Fold

  • What it is: A variation of K-Fold where each fold preserves the class proportions of the entire dataset.
  • When to use: Essential for imbalanced datasets (e.g., fraud detection with 99% negative and 1% positive cases), ensuring every fold has representative cases of the minority class.

Why Stratification Matters

In standard K-Fold, each fold is created by randomly partitioning data. When classes are imbalanced, random splits may produce folds that contain zero samples of the minority class. A model evaluated on such a fold would show misleadingly high accuracy (by predicting the majority class for every sample), hiding its inability to detect the minority class.

Fraud Detection Example: With 99% legitimate transactions and 1% fraudulent, a 5-fold random split could leave some folds with 0 fraud cases. Stratified K-Fold guarantees each fold has ~1% fraud samples, giving a realistic picture of model performance on the minority class.

When to Use Stratified K-Fold

  • Classification with imbalanced classes: Fraud detection, medical diagnosis, rare-event prediction.
  • Multi-class problems: Ensures every class appears in every fold.
  • Small datasets: Even minor imbalances are amplified with fewer samples.
Note: Stratification is not meaningful for regression tasks since the target is continuous. For regression, use standard K-Fold or shuffle-split strategies.
