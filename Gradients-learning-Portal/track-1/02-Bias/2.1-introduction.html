Module Overview & Structure

Welcome to the core curriculum for Module 02: Bias in Machine Learning. This module covers what bias is, why it leads to underfitting, and how it behaves within the Bias-Variance Tradeoff that guides every modeling decision. You will learn to diagnose high-bias models and apply practical fixes to your pipelines.

What is Generalization?

Generalization is the ability of a machine learning model to perform accurately on new, unseen data after being trained on a finite dataset. A model that generalizes well has learned the underlying patterns in the data rather than memorizing the training examples.

When a model fails to generalize, we observe one of two fundamental problems:

  • Underfitting (High Bias): The model is too simple to capture the true pattern â€” it performs poorly on both training and test data.
  • Overfitting (High Variance): The model is too complex and memorizes noise â€” it performs well on training data but poorly on test data.

The bias-variance tradeoff is the central challenge in achieving good generalization. This module explores the "bias" side of that tradeoff: what it is, how it manifests, when to accept it, and how to reduce it.

Key Insight: Throughout this module, remember that bias is not inherently bad â€” sometimes a simple, interpretable model with slight bias is preferable to a complex, uninterpretable one. The goal is informed tradeoff, not zero bias.
